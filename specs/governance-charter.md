# 1. Governance Charter (The Constitution)

This defines how the protocol is stewarded.

## 1.1 Purpose

To ensure the Fair Hiring Protocol remains:
- 	fair
- 	transparent
- 	stable
- 	community driven
- 	resistant to capture

## 1.2 Governance Bodies

As defined earlier:
- 	Protocol Council (PC)
- 	Technical Working Group (TWG)
- 	Fairness Oversight Board (FOB)

## 1.3 Authority Boundaries
- 	PC: approves protocol changes
- 	TWG: maintains implementation
- 	FOB: audits fairness and can veto unfair changes

## 1.4 Decision Rules
- 	4/6 PC majority for changes
- 	FOB veto overrides PC approval
- 	TWG cannot change fairness logic

## 1.5 Transparency Requirements
- 	all meetings public
- 	all decisions logged
- 	all proposals published
- 	all votes recorded

## 1.6 Anti Capture Rules
- 	no corporate sponsorship
- 	rotating membership
- 	term limits
- 	conflict of interest disclosures

*This is the backbone of trust.*

# 2. Proposal Format (FHP P)

This is how changes are proposed, reviewed, and approved.

Every proposal must follow a strict template:

## 2.1 Header
- 	Proposal ID: FHP-P-YYYY-NNN
- 	Title
- 	Author(s)
- 	Date submitted
- 	Category: schema / ontology / scoring / governance / documentation

## 2.2 Summary

A short explanation of the change.

## 2.3 Motivation

Why the change is needed.

## 2.4 Fairness Impact Assessment

This is mandatory and includes:
- 	who benefits
- 	who might be harmed
- 	whether bias could increase
- 	whether transparency could decrease
- 	whether candidate rights are affected

## 2.5 Technical Specification
Exact changes to:
- 	schemas
- 	scoring logic
- 	ontology
- 	governance rules

## 2.6 Backwards Compatibility

Does this break existing systems?

## 2.7 Alternatives Considered

What other options were rejected and why.

## 2.8 Security & Privacy Considerations

## 2.9 Implementation Plan

Steps, timeline, migration.

## 2.10 Appendix

Supporting data, diagrams, examples.

This ensures changes are thoughtful, slow, and fair.

# 3. Fairness Audit Process

This is how we ensure companies and the system behave fairly.

## 3.1 Audit Types
- 	Automated audits (continuous)
- 	Periodic audits (quarterly)
- 	Triggered audits (when bias is detected)

## 3.2 What Audits Check

### A. Job Brief Compliance
- 	salary range included
- 	no arbitrary filters
- 	must have skills realistic
- 	responsibilities clear

### B. Matching Fairness
- 	no demographic skew
- 	no unfair must have failures
- 	no unexplained rejections
- 	bias correction applied correctly

### C. Company Behavior
- 	no ghosting
- 	no fake jobs
- 	no fishing expeditions
- 	no manipulation of constraints

### D. System Behavior
- 	scoring consistent
- 	explanations generated
- 	logs complete
- 	no drift in fairness metrics

## 3.3 Audit Outcomes
- 	Pass
- 	Pass with warnings
- 	Fail (corrective action required)
- 	Fail (suspension)

## 3.4 Public Reporting
- 	anonymized fairness reports
- 	company level compliance badges
- 	protocol level fairness dashboards

*This is how fairness becomes enforceable.*

# 4. Company Compliance Requirements

Companies must meet these to use the system.

## 4.1 Mandatory Requirements
- 	provide salary ranges
- 	provide real responsibilities
- 	separate must have from nice to have
- 	no arbitrary filters
- 	no demographic filtering
- 	no keyword based exclusion
- 	no “years since graduation” filters
- 	no “culture fit” filters

## 4.2 Behavioral Requirements
- 	respond to all matched candidates
- 	provide structured rejection reasons
- 	adhere to fairness audits
- 	maintain accurate job briefs
- 	avoid fishing expeditions

## 4.3 Technical Requirements
- 	accept match explanations
- 	log decisions
- 	allow fairness monitoring
- 	use the canonical protocol version

## 4.4 Enforcement
Violations lead to:
- 	warnings
- 	job suspension
- 	company suspension
- 	public fairness violation report

*This ensures companies cannot quietly cheat.*

# 5. Candidate Rights Charter

This is the ethical heart of the system. Candidates have the right to:

## 5.1 Transparency
- 	see why they were matched
- 	see why they were rejected
- 	see how their score was computed
- 	see whether bias correction was applied

## 5.2 Control
- 	control visibility
- 	hide sensitive attributes
- 	correct inferred skills
- 	delete their profile
- 	export their data

## 5.3 Fair Treatment
- 	no arbitrary filtering
- 	no discrimination
- 	no ghosting
- 	no opaque decisions

## 5.4 Appeal
- 	challenge incorrect inferences
- 	challenge unfair rejections
- 	request audit review

## 5.5 Privacy
- 	minimal data retention
- 	no surveillance
- 	no behavioral profiling
- 	no unauthorized data sharing

*This is what makes the system humane.*
