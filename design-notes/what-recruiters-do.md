# Upend the madness

I was thinking of upending the recruitment industry. They don't do well at matching people. Plenty of friends can't get jobs due to discrimination, such as age. 

The goal is finding a way to do it altruistically (to help people & companies), rather than commercially - i.e. minimal cost. 

*What does a recruiter actually do aside from glance at a few people's cv and say "no"?*

This will hit a nerve because we’re pointing at a real structural failure in the recruitment ecosystem.  I am not imagining the dysfunction. I am diagnosing it. I'll break this down in a way that helps us see the opportunity, the pitfalls, and the architecture we’re implicitly reaching for.

# What recruiters actually do (beyond the caricature)

A good recruiter does far more than skim CVs. A bad recruiter does exactly what I described. The problem is that the industry incentives reward the latter.

Here’s the real job, stripped of fluff:

## 1. Intake & translation
- Understand what the hiring manager really wants (which is often not what the job description says).
- Translate fuzzy human requirements into something matchable.

## 2. Sourcing
- Find candidates through:
	-	LinkedIn
	-	CV databases
	-	Their own network
	-	Referrals
	-	Cold outreach

## 3. Filtering
- Quick CV triage (often biased, rushed, and inconsistent)
- Keyword matching
- Gut-feel heuristics (this is where discrimination creeps in)

## 4. Selling
- Sell the candidate to the company
- Sell the company to the candidate

## 5. Process management
- Scheduling
- Nudging
- Keeping both sides warm
- Negotiating salary
- Closing the offer

## 6. Risk management
- Avoiding bad hires
- Avoiding candidates who will drop out
- Avoiding reputational damage

Most of this is coordination, translation, and salesmanship, not matching.

*Matching is the part that AI can do better than humans — and the part humans currently do worst.*

# Why the industry is broken

Because the incentives are misaligned:
- Recruiters get paid when someone is hired, not when the right person is hired.
- They optimize for speed, not accuracy.
- They are overloaded (100+ roles, thousands of applicants).
- They rely on heuristics that amplify bias.
- They gatekeep based on arbitrary signals (age, job titles, prestige).

*The system is fundamentally incapable of fairness.*
