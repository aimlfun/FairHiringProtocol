#  Fair Hiring Protocol — Governance Model (Draft v1.0)

Governance is where we define:
- 	who maintains the protocol
- 	how fairness rules evolve
- 	how disputes are handled
- 	how companies are held accountable
- 	how the community contributes
- 	how updates are approved

Governance is where fairness either becomes real or becomes theoretical. A fair system needs a fair constitution, but it also needs a fair parliament, fair courts, and fair enforcement. Otherwise the protocol drifts, companies game it, or well meaning contributors accidentally break fairness.

Below is a clean, structured governance model tailored specifically for the **Fair Hiring Protocol (FHP)**.

# A governance system designed to protect fairness, prevent capture, and ensure long term integrity

We’ll break it into:
1.	Governance Principles
2.	Governance Bodies
3.	Change Process
4.	Fairness Enforcement
5.	Dispute Resolution
6.	Anti Capture Safeguards
7.	Public Transparency Requirements

*This is the backbone of the entire ecosystem.*

# 1. Governance Principles

These are the non negotiables — the constitution.

## 1.1. Fairness First

Every decision must prioritize fairness over:
- 	commercial interests
- 	speed
- 	convenience
- 	company pressure

## 1.2. Transparency

All governance decisions must be:
- 	public
- 	documented
- 	versioned
- 	auditable

## 1.3. No Centralized Power

No single company, government, or individual can control the protocol.

## 1.4. Candidate Protection

The protocol exists primarily to protect candidates, not companies.

## 1.5. Stability

Changes must be:
- 	slow
- 	deliberate
- 	backwards compatible

## 1.6. Inclusivity

Governance must include:
- 	technical experts
- 	fairness experts
- 	candidates
- 	employers
- 	community contributors

# 2. Governance Bodies

We define three bodies with clear, limited powers.

## 2.1. The Protocol Council (PC)

Role: Stewards the Fair Hiring Protocol.

Composition:
- 	1 fairness expert
- 	1 legal expert (employment law)
- 	1 technical architect
- 	1 candidate representative
- 	1 employer representative
- 	1 independent ethics advisor

Powers:
- 	approve or reject protocol changes
- 	maintain the canonical version
- 	enforce fairness rules
- 	oversee audits

Cannot:
- 	change scoring weights unilaterally
- 	override fairness thresholds
- 	accept corporate sponsorship

## 2.2. The Technical Working Group (TWG)

Role: Maintains schemas, ontology, and reference implementation.

Composition: Open membership, merit based contribution.

Powers:
- 	propose improvements
- 	maintain code
- 	update ontology
- 	fix bugs

Cannot:
- 	change fairness rules
- 	change governance rules
- 	change bias thresholds

## 2.3. The Fairness Oversight Board (FOB)

Role: Independent watchdog.

Composition:
- 	fairness researchers
- 	civil rights advocates
- 	disability advocates
- 	neurodiversity advocates
- 	labor representatives

Powers:
- 	audit companies
- 	audit the protocol
- 	publish fairness reports
- 	veto changes that reduce fairness

Cannot:
- 	modify technical implementation
- 	influence scoring weights directly

# 3. Change Process

Changes must be slow, deliberate, and transparent.

## 3.1. Proposal (FHP P)

Anyone can submit a proposal:
- 	schema change
- 	ontology update
- 	scoring adjustment
- 	governance improvement

## 3.2. Public Review

All proposals undergo:
- 	30 day public comment
- 	fairness impact assessment
- 	technical feasibility review

## 3.3. Council Vote

Protocol Council votes:
- 	4/6 majority required
- 	Fairness Oversight Board has veto power

## 3.4. Versioning

Changes follow semantic versioning:
- 	MAJOR: fairness logic changes
- 	MINOR: schema/ontology updates
- 	PATCH: clarifications

# 4. Fairness Enforcement

This is where governance becomes real.

## 4.1. Compliance Certification

Companies must:
- 	pass fairness audits
- 	adhere to protocol
- 	provide transparency logs

## 4.2. Violations

If a company:
- 	manipulates job briefs
- 	uses forbidden filters
- 	ignores fairness corrections
- 	misrepresents roles

Then the system can:
- 	suspend the job
- 	suspend the company
- 	publish a fairness violation report

## 4.3. Automatic Enforcement
The matching engine itself enforces:
- 	no arbitrary filters
- 	mandatory salary ranges
- 	constraint transparency
- 	explanation generation

*Governance ensures the rules stay intact.*

# 5. Dispute Resolution

We need a fair way to handle disagreements.

## 5.1. Candidate Appeals

Candidates can appeal:
- 	unfair rejections
- 	incorrect inferences
- 	bias concerns

The system must:
- 	review logs
- 	provide explanations
- 	correct errors

## 5.2. Company Appeals

Companies can appeal:
- 	fairness flags
- 	job suspensions
- 	audit findings

Handled by:
- 	Protocol Council
- 	with Fairness Board oversight

# 6. Anti Capture Safeguards

This is critical. We must prevent:
- 	corporate capture
- 	political capture
- 	ideological capture
- 	technical monoculture

## 6.1. No Corporate Sponsorship

Funding must be:
- 	transparent
- 	diversified
- 	capped per entity

## 6.2. Rotating Membership

Council and Board members rotate every 2 years.

## 6.3. Open Meetings

All governance meetings are public.

## 6.4. Public Logs

All decisions are logged and published.

## 6.5. Multi Model Validation

No single AI model can dominate the system.

# 7. Public Transparency Requirements

The protocol must publish:
- 	fairness metrics
- 	audit results
- 	version history
- 	governance decisions
- 	proposed changes
- 	rejected proposals
- 	rationale for decisions

*This is how we build trust.*

# Why This Governance Model Works

It is:
- 	resistant to capture
- 	transparent
- 	slow to change (good for fairness)
- 	inclusive
- 	auditable
- 	candidate protective
- 	company accountable
- 	community driven

It mirrors the governance of:
- 	W3C
- 	IETF
- 	Linux Foundation
- 	Open Banking
- 	GDPR working groups

*But adapted for fairness critical hiring.*
