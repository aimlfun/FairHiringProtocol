# Structure Rejectio Reasons & Appeals

Structured rejection reasons and appeals are two of the most powerful fairness levers in the entire protocol. 
They turn hiring from a black box into a dialogue with accountability.

Let’s take them one at a time, starting with the taxonomy.

# 1. Structured Rejection Reason Taxonomy (FHP SRR v1.0)

This taxonomy must be:
- finite (no free text loopholes)
- non-discriminatory
- auditable
- explainable
- aligned with the scoring pipeline
- safe for candidates
- safe for companies
- useful for fairness monitoring

The taxonomy is divided into **four categories**, each with specific allowed codes.

## Category A — Must Have Skill Gaps

These are the only skill based rejections allowed.
- A1_missing_must_have_skill
- A2_insufficient_proficiency
- A3_missing_required_certification
- A4_missing_required_license

**Why this matters:** These map directly to the must have evaluation stage, so they are explainable and auditable.

## Category B — Constraint Mismatches

These are objective, non discriminatory constraints.
- B1_location_not_viable
- B2_salary_misaligned
- B3_work_mode_misaligned
- B4_legal_authorization_missing
- B5_availability_misaligned

**Why this matters:** These map directly to the constraint evaluator stage.

## Category C — Experience & Responsibility Gaps

These must be tied to the job brief’s responsibilities.
- C1_insufficient_responsibility_alignment
- C2_insufficient_domain_experience
- C3_insufficient_seniority
- C4_insufficient_leadership_experience

**Why this matters:** These map to the experience relevance stage.

## Category D — Preference Misalignment

These are candidate preferences, not company preferences.
- D1_candidate_salary_preference_mismatch
- D2_candidate_work_mode_preference_mismatch
- D3_candidate_role_preference_mismatch

**Why this matters:** These map to the preference alignment stage.

## Forbidden Rejection Reasons

These are explicitly banned:
- “culture fit”
- “overqualified”
- “underqualified” (without specifics)
- “not a good match”
- “not the right background”
- “not enough experience” (without mapping to responsibilities)
- “recent graduate”
- “too senior”
- “too junior”
- “years since graduation”
- “years of experience” as a proxy for age
- anything referencing demographics

These are the phrases companies use today to hide bias. FHP eliminates them.

## How companies use the taxonomy

When responding to a match:
```
{
  "status": "rejected",
  "reason_code": "C1_insufficient_responsibility_alignment",
  "reason_details": {
    "responsibility": "own production reliability",
    "missing_evidence": "no on-call or incident response experience"
  }
}
```

This is:
- structured
- explainable
- auditable
- respectful
- fair

And the candidate sees a clear explanation.

# 2. Appeals — Who They’re For

Appeals exist for both candidates and companies, but for different reasons.

Let’s break it down.

## A. Candidate Appeals

Candidates can appeal when:
- an inference is wrong
- a skill was misinterpreted
- a responsibility was mis mapped
- a constraint was mis evaluated
- a rejection reason is incorrect
- bias correction was not applied when it should have been
- the company ghosted them
- the company used an invalid rejection reason

Candidate appeal flow:
1.	Candidate requests /audit/match/{match_id}
2.	Candidate submits appeal with evidence
3.	System re runs the match with corrected data
4.	Governance reviews if needed
5.	Company may be flagged if misuse is detected

Appeals protect candidates from:
- model errors
- unfair rejections
- discriminatory behaviour
- sloppy job briefs

## B. Company Appeals

Companies can appeal when:
- a job was suspended
- a fairness violation was issued
- a bias correction was applied incorrectly
- a candidate appeal triggered a governance review
- the ontology mapping is disputed
- the scoring interpretation is disputed

Company appeal flow:
1.	Company submits appeal with evidence
2.	Governance reviews
3.	Decision is logged publicly
4.	Ontology or scoring may be updated (via FHP P proposal)

Appeals protect companies from:
- false positives
- ontology errors
- model drift
- misinterpretation of job briefs

## C. Governance Appeals (Meta Appeals)

Governance bodies can appeal:
- ontology changes
- scoring changes
- bias thresholds
- enforcement actions

*This ensures the protocol itself stays fair.*

# 3. Why Appeals Matter

Appeals are the pressure release valve that keeps the system fair without being rigid.

They ensure:
- candidates aren’t harmed by model mistakes
- companies aren’t harmed by false bias signals
- governance can correct systemic issues
- the protocol evolves safely

*Appeals are not a sign of failure — they’re a sign of maturity.*

# 4. How Appeals Interact with the Rejection Taxonomy

This is where the magic happens:
- If a company uses a forbidden rejection reason → automatic violation
- If a company uses a valid reason but the trace contradicts it → candidate appeal upheld
- If a candidate claims a skill was mis inferred → multi model layer re runs
- If a company claims bias correction was misapplied → governance reviews metrics
- If a pattern emerges → ontology or scoring may be updated

*The taxonomy + appeals create a closed fairness loop.*
