# Fair Hiring Protocol — Governance Model

This document defines how the Fair Hiring Protocol (FHP) is governed.  

It ensures fairness, transparency, stability, and resistance to capture.

---

# 1. Governance Principles

- **Fairness first**  
- **Transparency in all decisions**  
- **Candidate protection**  
- **Community-driven evolution**  
- **No single point of control**  
- **Slow, deliberate change**  
- **Anti-capture safeguards**

---

# 2. Governance Bodies

## 2.1 Protocol Council (PC)
**Role:** Stewards the protocol.  

**Composition:**  
- 1 fairness expert  
- 1 legal expert  
- 1 technical architect  
- 1 candidate representative  
- 1 employer representative  
- 1 independent ethics advisor  

**Powers:**  
- Approve/reject protocol changes  
- Maintain canonical version  
- Oversee audits  

**Limitations:**  
- Cannot change fairness thresholds  
- Cannot accept corporate sponsorship  

---

## 2.2 Technical Working Group (TWG)
**Role:** Maintains schemas, ontology, and reference implementation.  

**Open membership.**

**Powers:**  
- Propose improvements  
- Maintain code  
- Update ontology  

**Limitations:**  
- Cannot modify fairness logic  
- Cannot modify governance rules  

---

## 2.3 Fairness Oversight Board (FOB)
**Role:** Independent watchdog.  

**Composition:**  
- fairness researchers  
- civil rights advocates  
- disability/neurodiversity advocates  
- labor representatives  

**Powers:**  
- Audit fairness  
- Publish reports  
- Veto unfair changes  

---

# 3. Change Process (FHP P)

All changes must follow the FHP P proposal process:

1. Submit proposal in `/proposals`  
2. 30 day public review  
3. Fairness impact assessment  
4. TWG technical review  
5. PC vote (4/6 majority)  
6. FOB veto window  
7. Version update  

---

# 4. Anti Capture Safeguards

- No corporate sponsorship  
- Term limits for PC and FOB  
- Public meetings  
- Public logs  
- Multi model validation  
- Conflict of interest disclosures  

---

# 5. Transparency Requirements

- All decisions public  
- All votes recorded  
- All proposals archived  
- All audits published  

---

# 6. Enforcement

The governance model is binding.  

Violations may result in removal from governance roles.
